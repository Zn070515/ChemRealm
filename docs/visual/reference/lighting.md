# Lighting and reflection-card reference Bible

ChemRealm's glass language is a scene-level relationship between material,
background, camera, and reflected cards. A shader parameter alone is not the
visual style.

## GOOD

- One readable key area, a controlled fill, and a narrow edge/reflection card
  whose purpose is recorded.
- Reflection cards outside the camera view that create a soft bright edge or a
  quiet dark separation without becoming visible set dressing.
- Enough background contrast to show the rim and spout while preserving a
  neutral apparatus body.
- Light that preserves graduation and marking hierarchy rather than washing it
  out.
- Stable orthographic framing so differences are attributable to the declared
  lighting variable.

## BAD

- A generic three-light recipe copied to every apparatus.
- A bright outline that is really a light leak rather than a structural edge.
- A dark-background-only treatment that disappears on the default light view.
- A high-contrast product shot that looks attractive at full size but loses the
  silhouette at thumbnail size.
- Reflection cards that make the glass look metallic, wet, or candy-coated.

## Controlled study rigs

The study must compare named rigs rather than anonymous light values:

- `soft-neutral`: balanced key/fill, moderate edge readability;
- `edge-card`: restrained key/fill with a deliberate narrow edge card;
- `quiet-studio`: lower contrast and broader response for neutral teaching
  scenes.

These names describe study intent, not permanent production style. The owner
may reject all three.

## Do not copy

Do not copy a photographed studio's exact light placement or product-brand
look. The cards are an authored technical device for reproducing a visual cue,
not a claim that the virtual lab has measured the physical environment.
